Step 1 · Goal

Increase local brand awareness.

Our audit found that Welch Ave. Station's biggest problem isn't its prices. It's that people can't see them. The website hides its best selling points (no cover, the lowest regular prices, game-day specials) behind weak navigation and a poor mobile experience.

How we'll measure it

  • Reach and impressions in the Ames area (awareness)
  • Click-through rate against the 0.46% to 0.52% display benchmark
  • Landing-page actions: Get directions, tap to call, or open a day's specials, tagged by ad size with UTM links
  • Cost per action against our $18.57 expected-case estimate
Step 1 · Targeting

Who we're talking to.

Primary

Budget-conscious college students

Iowa State students, 21 and older, looking for affordable nightlife. Won by "no cover charge all night" and the cheapest regular prices at Campustown bars.

Secondary

Casual socializers & sports fans

People who want a relaxed spot, not a dance club. They value the capacity limit, big-screen TVs for game day, and free pool and darts.

Niche

Local service-industry workers

Bartenders, servers and hospitality staff in Ames, reached by Sunday S.I.N. (Service Industry Night) and its employee pricing.

Step 1 · The hook

"No cover. No catch."

On a big night out, cover charges add up fast. Welch doesn't charge one, and its regular prices are the lowest in Campustown. The hook says both things in four words, then the landing page delivers the proof.

Core messaging

  • Headline: No cover. No catch.
  • Proof: Campustown's lowest regular prices and a deal every night of the week
  • Reasons to stay: Free pool Monday through Wednesday, the game on the big screens, Pizza Pit at the bar
  • Call to action: See tonight's specials
  • Voice: Welcoming, straight-shooting and relaxed, like your favorite local bartender
  • Look: The real bartender in the real blue polo, the mural's painted banner, and gold for game day

Assumptions

Conservative assumes a $4.00 CPM, because tight 21+ local targeting usually costs more than the network average (our estimate). Expected uses the 2.9% landing-page action rate from the benchmark and a $2.80 CPM, which is realistic for a strong local display buy. Optimistic assumes more efficient placement and a conversion rate near competitor restaurant benchmarks.

Sanity check

The expected cost per click ($0.54) lands right next to the $0.63 Google Display Network average, so the plan is realistic. At $150 a week, the expected case delivers about 53,600 impressions and 278 clicks per week, enough to build local awareness without overspending.

Step 3 · Landing page

Click the ad, land in the same place.

The landing page repeats the ad word for word, so nobody wonders if they clicked the right thing.

  • Same headline: "No cover. No catch." is the landing page's H1
  • Same offer and button: "See tonight's specials" opens today's specials automatically
  • Same look: polo-royal blue, the mural's painted banner, gold buttons, the same bartender photo
  • Fixes from our audit: mobile-friendly, served over HTTPS on GitHub Pages, a nav bar, the Pizza Pit menu as real text, the phone and address on every screen
